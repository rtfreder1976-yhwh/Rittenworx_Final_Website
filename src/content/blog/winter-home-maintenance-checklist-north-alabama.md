North Alabama winters are mild until they aren't. A few nights in the teens, a week of cold rain, and the small problems you ignored in October turn into repair calls in January. This is the short list worth doing before the first hard freeze, in order of what costs the most when it's missed.

## 1. Protect the pipes first

Water damage is the expensive one. Before a cold snap:

- Disconnect garden hoses and shut off exterior spigots from the inside valve if your house has one.
- Open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.
- Let a faucet drip slightly on the coldest nights if a line runs through an unheated garage, crawlspace or attic.
- Find your main water shutoff now, not when something is spraying. Everyone in the house should know where it is.

If a pipe does burst, shut off the water, then read our guide to [what to do when you find water damage behind drywall](/blog/what-to-do-when-you-find-water-damage-behind-drywall). You'll want a licensed plumber for the pipe itself.

## 2. Clear the gutters after leaf drop

Gutters packed with wet leaves hold water against the fascia and overflow down the siding. Once the leaves are down, get them cleaned out and check that downspouts discharge away from the foundation. Our [gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning) page covers what we do, and the [overflowing gutters guide](/blog/what-to-do-when-your-gutters-are-overflowing) explains what to look for.

## 3. Walk the exterior for gaps

Cold, wet weather finds every gap. Look at:

- Caulk around windows, door trim and where siding meets trim. Cracked or missing caulk is an open door for water.
- Weatherstripping and door sweeps. If you can see daylight under an exterior door, air and moisture are getting in.
- Peeling paint on trim and fascia. Bare wood absorbs water and rots. See [what to do when exterior paint starts peeling](/blog/what-to-do-when-exterior-paint-starts-peeling).

## 4. Check the ceilings and walls inside

Look for new stains, bubbling paint or hairline cracks, especially under bathrooms, along exterior walls and below the roofline. A stain that grows after rain is an active leak, not a cosmetic problem. Small stable cracks and nail pops are normal seasonal movement and are an easy [drywall repair](/services/drywall) once the house stops shifting.

## 5. Test the safety basics

Replace smoke and carbon monoxide detector batteries, and test them. Heating season is when CO risk goes up.
