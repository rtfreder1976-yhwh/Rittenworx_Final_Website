A wood fence is one of those projects where the work you can't see decides how long it lasts. Panels are easy to swap. Posts set wrong lean within a couple of years, and then the whole run goes with them. Here's what's worth settling before anyone starts digging in a Huntsville yard.

## 1. Pick the Style for the Job

- **Privacy fence (6 ft is the common height):** solid board-on-board or shadowbox. The usual choice for backyards, pools, and dogs.
- **Picket fence (3 to 4 ft):** marks a front yard without walling it off.
- **Split-rail:** simple posts and rails for large lots and acreage. It marks a line without blocking the view.

Shadowbox and board-on-board look the same from both sides, which neighbors appreciate. A single-sided fence should have its finished side facing out. Many HOAs require that.

## 2. Cedar or Pressure-Treated Pine

- **Pressure-treated pine** costs less up front and does fine if it's stained or sealed every few years.
- **Cedar** costs more, but resists rot and insects naturally and weathers to a soft gray if you leave it unfinished.

In North Alabama's humidity, the posts take the most punishment whichever wood you pick, because they sit in wet ground. Make sure the posts are rated for ground contact, even if the pickets are cedar.

## 3. Know the Line, the Rules, and What's Underground

Three things to handle before any digging:

- **Property line.** If you have a survey or plat from closing, find it. A fence a foot onto a neighbor's lot is an expensive mistake to undo. Many people set the fence a few inches inside their own line on purpose.
- **HOA and city rules.** A lot of Huntsville and Madison subdivisions have rules on fence height, material, color, and which side faces out, and some require approval before you build. Check the HOA covenants, and check with the city about permit requirements for the height you want.
- **Call 811 before you dig.** Alabama 811 will mark buried utility lines for free. Post holes go deep enough to hit gas, power, or fiber lines.

## 4. Setting Posts in Clay

Much of Huntsville sits on dense red clay that holds water. That matters for posts:

- **Depth:** a common rule of thumb is about a third of the post's above-ground height buried, deeper for gate posts.
- **Concrete footings:** set each post plumb in concrete, with a little gravel at the bottom of the hole so water drains away from the end grain.
- **Shape the top of the footing** so water runs off the post instead of pooling around it. Water sitting against wood at ground level is where rot starts.
- **Gate posts** carry the most load. They get the deepest holes, and the concrete needs to cure before the gate is hung.

## 5. Finishing and Upkeep

New pressure-treated wood often needs to dry out for a while before it takes stain well. Once it's finished, a quick walk of the fence line each fall catches problems early: wobbly posts, rot at the base, and gates that drag. Our [fall prep checklist](/blog/fall-prep-pressure-washing-deck-fence-north-alabama) covers what to look for.

## The Short Version

Decide style and wood, confirm the property line and HOA rules, call 811, then put the effort into the posts. Everything above ground can be fixed later. Posts set wrong usually mean starting over.
