Fence quotes in Decatur can swing a lot from one company to the next, and it's rarely because someone's trying to overcharge you. Wood fencing is priced by the linear foot, and a handful of factors on your specific property move that per-foot number more than people expect. Here's what actually goes into the number.

## Fence Type Sets the Base Price

- **Split-rail** is the cheapest per foot — it's just posts and rails, no pickets or panels to mill and hang. Good for marking a large lot without blocking the view.
- **Picket** costs more than split-rail because of the picket material and spacing labor, but less than privacy fencing since the boards are shorter and there's less total wood.
- **Privacy** (board-on-board or shadowbox) is the most expensive style. Full 6-ft or 8-ft boards, tighter spacing, and more material per linear foot add up fast.

## Cedar vs. Pressure-Treated Pine

Pressure-treated pine is the budget option and holds up fine if you stain or seal it every few years. Cedar costs noticeably more per board but naturally resists rot and bugs, and it ages to a nice silver-gray if you leave it unfinished. On a full backyard privacy fence, that material choice alone can be the difference of a few hundred to over a thousand dollars depending on total footage.

## What Your Yard Does to the Price

This is the part people underestimate. Two houses with the same fence style and length can get very different quotes because of what's actually in the ground:

- **Clay and rock.** A lot of Decatur lots have hard clay or shallow rock a foot or two down. Digging post holes by hand in that ground takes real time; an auger helps but doesn't eliminate the labor.
- **Slope.** A fence line running across a grade needs stepped panels or racked (angled) rails to follow the ground without leaving a gap underneath. That's more layout work than a flat, straight run.
- **Tree roots.** Established trees near the property line mean working around roots instead of through them, which slows digging and sometimes shifts post placement.

## Gates Aren't Free

Every gate adds hardware, hinges, and a latch — plus the framing to keep it from sagging over time. A single walk gate is a modest add. A double drive gate wide enough for a mower or trailer costs more, both in material and in the extra bracing it needs to stay square.

## Repair vs. Replace

If only a few posts or a section of panels are rotted, repairing that section is almost always cheaper than a full rebuild — and faster, since there's no need to pull an entire fence line. The math changes once rot shows up on multiple posts spread across the run. At that point you're paying labor to fix each spot individually, and a rebuild often ends up costing about the same while giving you a fence that's straight and solid start to finish. ## What's Usually Included in a Written Quote

- Materials (posts, rails, pickets or panels, concrete)
- Post-hole digging and concrete footings
- Panel, picket, or rail installation
- Gates and hardware
- Property line confirmation before any digging starts
- Cleanup and haul-off of old fencing, if applicable

## Before You Sign Anything

Confirm your property line first — either from a survey or plat map, or by walking it with your contractor before any holes get dug. And check whether your subdivision's HOA has rules on fence height, material, or style; some Decatur neighborhoods do, and finding out after the fence is up is a much worse conversation than finding out before.
