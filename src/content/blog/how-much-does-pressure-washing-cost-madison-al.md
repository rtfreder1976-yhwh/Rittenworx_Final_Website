Pressure washing sounds like a simple service, so the price range people see when they search for it can be confusing — a driveway wash and a full house-plus-roof job aren't the same category of work. Here's what actually moves the number.

## Surface Type Matters More Than Square Footage

Not every surface gets the same treatment, and that changes both the method and the price:

- **Concrete (driveways, walkways, patios)** can take real pressure. This is usually the fastest, most straightforward washing to price — higher PSI lifts oil stains, tire marks, and ground-in dirt without any risk to the surface.
- **Siding (vinyl, Hardie board, brick)** needs a soft wash — low pressure plus a cleaning solution — instead of a blast. Full pressure can force water behind siding panels or crack them, so this is slower, more careful work than driveway washing, which is reflected in the price.
- **Decks and fences** get washed as prep before staining or sealing, which means the wood needs to come out clean without the wash stripping or damaging it — another soft-wash job, often bundled with staining as one project.
- **Roofs** are the most specialized surface. A shingle roof should never see high pressure — it strips the protective granules that protect against UV and weather. Roof washing is always a soft wash, and the care required (plus the equipment for working at height) makes it the highest per-surface rate.

## Buildup Level Changes the Job

A house that's never been washed and has heavy mildew, green algae streaking, or years of pollen film needs pre-treatment — a cleaning solution applied and given time to work before the actual wash. That extra step adds time and materials to a job compared to a lighter annual cleaning. If you're on a shaded, wooded lot that stays damp, expect to be on the higher end of that range more consistently than a home in full sun.

## Bundling Saves You Money

Most of our pressure washing jobs in Madison aren't just one surface — they're a house-plus-driveway, or a deck wash paired with staining, or exterior washing done right before a repaint. Bundling multiple surfaces into one visit is more efficient for us than separate trips, and that efficiency is reflected in the price when you do it all at once instead of calling three separate times through the year.

## How Often Madison Homes Actually Need It

Once a year is typical for most homes here, given North Alabama's humidity and heavy spring pollen. If your lot is shaded or backs up to woods, algae and mildew come back faster, and washing every 6-9 months makes more sense than waiting the full year and dealing with a heavier, more expensive clean each time.

## What's Included in a Written Estimate

- The surfaces being washed and the method for each (soft wash vs. standard pressure)
- Whether pre-treatment is needed for heavy buildup
- Any masking or protection needed for plants, outlets, or windows
- A final walkthrough to confirm everything came out the way you expected

## One Thing Worth Knowing Before You Rent Equipment Yourself

If you're tempted to rent a pressure washer and do it yourself, the surface-matching is the part that trips people up — full pressure on siding or a roof causes damage that costs far more to fix than the wash would have cost in the first place. If you're not sure which setting is safe for which surface, that's the reason to call rather than guess.
