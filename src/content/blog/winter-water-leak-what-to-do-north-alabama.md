When water is coming through a ceiling or out of a wall, what you do in the first hour matters more than anything after. Here is the order that limits the damage for homes in Madison, Huntsville, Decatur, Athens and the towns around them.

## The first 15 minutes

1. **Shut off the water.** Turn off the main valve if the leak is from a supply line. If you can't find it, that's the first thing to fix once this is over.
2. **Cut power to the wet area.** If water is near outlets, light fixtures or a ceiling fan, turn off those breakers. Don't stand in standing water to do it. If you can't reach the panel safely, call an electrician.
3. **Move what you can.** Furniture, rugs and electronics out of the drip zone. Put a bucket under active drips.
4. **Take photos and video** of everything before you touch it. You'll want them for an insurance claim.

## Who to call

- **Burst or leaking pipe:** a licensed plumber fixes the pipe. That's not our trade.
- **Large or widespread water:** call your insurance company early. Ask what they require before you start tearing things out.
- **Water near the electrical panel or lots of wet ceiling:** treat it as an emergency and get the right trade out the same day.

## Start the drying clock

Water-damaged areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours, according to the EPA, to prevent mold growth. Run fans and a dehumidifier, and open the wall or ceiling if it's soaked. Sagging, bubbling or soft drywall can't be saved and should come out.

Do not paint or caulk over a stain. Painting over a wet or moldy wall seals the problem in.
