"How much will it cost?" is the first question almost everyone asks, and it's a fair one. The honest answer is that it depends on the job, which isn't much help on its own. So here's what it actually depends on. Once you know these, you'll understand why two jobs that sound the same can be priced differently, and you'll know what to tell a handyman so the quote you get is the one you pay.

## 1. Scope: How Much Is Actually Being Done

This is the biggest factor by far. Painting "a bedroom" can mean rolling one accent wall or doing walls, ceiling, trim, doors, and closet. "Fix the fence" can mean one post or a whole run.

The more specific you are up front, the closer the quote will be. A list helps: what you want done, room by room or item by item.

## 2. Prep and Repair Before the Visible Work

A lot of what you're paying for happens before the part you see. Paint goes on in a few hours; patching, sanding, caulking, and priming the surface so the paint looks right takes longer. New flooring goes in quickly over a flat subfloor, but not over one that dips or squeaks.

If a wall has a lot of patched areas, if trim is cracked, or if the subfloor needs work, that's part of the job, and a good quote will say so.

## 3. Materials

Some jobs are mostly labor. Others are mostly materials. Things that change material cost:

- **Product choice.** Cedar versus pressure-treated pine for a fence. Different flooring lines. Paint grades.
- **Quantity.** Square footage, linear footage, number of doors or fixtures.
- **Who's buying.** Some homeowners like to pick up their own fixtures or flooring. That's usually fine, but tell your handyman up front, since it changes the quote and who's responsible if a part is wrong.

## 4. Access and Setup

Where the work is matters almost as much as what it is:

- A two-story exterior needs taller ladders or staging and more time per foot than a single-story ranch.
- A crawlspace or attic job is slower than the same work at standing height.
- Furniture that needs to be moved, or a room that has to stay usable during the job, adds time.
- Brick, block, and tile walls take different tools and anchors than drywall. A TV going on a brick fireplace isn't the same job as one going on a bedroom wall.

## 5. What's Hidden

This is the factor nobody can fully price ahead of time. Pull off a rotted piece of exterior trim and the sheathing behind it may be soft too. Open up a water-stained ceiling and the leak may have spread further than the stain.

A trustworthy quote handles this up front. It should say what's included, what might come up, and how you'll be told before anything extra gets done. Anyone who promises there's no chance of surprises on a rot or water job either hasn't looked closely or isn't being straight with you.

## 6. Bundling Small Jobs

Every trip has setup and travel time built in, whether the job takes twenty minutes or three hours. That's why a list of five small things usually makes more sense than five separate calls. If you've got a running list (a sticking door, a loose towel bar, a hole where the old TV mount was), send it all at once.

## How to Get a Quote That Holds

- **Send photos.** A wide shot, a close-up, and something for scale. Our guide on [what photos to text for a faster quote](/blog/what-photos-to-send-for-a-handyman-quote) goes job by job.
- **Be specific about scope.** Rooms, items, and what "done" looks like to you.
- **Ask what's not included.** Materials, disposal, patching, painting after a repair.
- **Get it in writing.** A written estimate is easier for both sides to stick to than a number said in the driveway.
