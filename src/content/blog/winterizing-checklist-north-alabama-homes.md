North Alabama winters are mostly mild, which is exactly why they catch people off guard. We go weeks in the 40s and 50s, then a cold front drops overnight lows into the teens for two or three days. Homes here aren't built for long freezes, so a short one can do real damage: a split pipe in an exterior wall, an overflowing gutter that turns into an ice dam on the porch, or a drafty door that runs the heat pump all night.

This checklist is ordered by what costs the most if you skip it.

## 1. Protect Exposed Pipes

Burst pipes are the most expensive winter problem we see, and almost all of them are preventable.

- **Disconnect garden hoses** from every outdoor spigot. A hose left attached traps water in the faucet, and that's where it freezes and splits.
- **Cover outdoor spigots** with foam faucet covers. They cost a few dollars at any hardware store.
- **Insulate pipes in crawlspaces, garages, and exterior walls** with foam pipe sleeves. Pay special attention to pipes on north-facing walls.
- **Know where your main water shutoff is** and make sure it turns. If a pipe bursts, every minute the water runs means more drywall and flooring to replace.
- **On nights in the teens**, let faucets on exterior walls drip and open the cabinet doors under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes.

If a pipe does burst, shut off the water and call a licensed plumber for the pipe itself. Once it's fixed, we handle what comes after: cutting out wet drywall, drying it out, replacing it, and repainting. ## 2. Clean the Gutters After the Leaves Drop

In most of North Alabama, the last big leaf drop is late November. Gutters cleaned in October are often full again by Thanksgiving. Clogged gutters in winter hold water against the fascia, overflow onto walkways where it freezes, and can push water back under the shingles.

Clean them once the trees are bare, and check that downspouts carry water at least a few feet away from the foundation. More on that in our guide to [overflowing gutters](/blog/what-to-do-when-your-gutters-are-overflowing), or see our [gutter cleaning service](/services/gutter-cleaning).

## 3. Seal the Drafts

Gaps around doors and windows are where most heat escapes in older homes, and they're cheap to fix.

- **Door sweeps:** if you can see daylight under an exterior door, the sweep is worn or missing.
- **Weatherstripping:** close the door on a dollar bill. If it slides out easily, the weatherstripping isn't sealing.
- **Exterior caulk:** check around window and door trim for cracked or missing caulk. Gaps here let in both cold air and water.

We wrote a full guide on [finding and fixing drafty doors and windows](/blog/drafty-doors-windows-weatherstripping-caulking-north-alabama).

## 4. Walk the Deck and Fence

Wood that's soft now gets worse through a wet winter. Push a screwdriver into the base of deck posts and fence posts. If it sinks in easily, that wood is rotting. Fixing a few boards or a post now is far cheaper than rebuilding a section in spring. Our [fall deck and fence guide](/blog/fall-prep-pressure-washing-deck-fence-north-alabama) covers what to look for.

## 5. Check Smoke and CO Detectors

Heating season is when carbon monoxide risk is highest, especially in homes with gas furnaces, water heaters, or fireplaces. Test every detector, replace batteries, and replace any detector older than 10 years (the date is printed on the back).

## The Short Version

If you only have one Saturday: disconnect the hoses, cover the spigots, insulate any exposed pipes, and clean the gutters once the leaves are down. Those four things prevent most of the winter damage calls we get.
