A wall-mounted TV frees up floor space and makes a room look finished. A badly mounted one sags, sits too high, or has a cord dangling down the wall that bugs you every time you look at it. Most of the difference comes down to a few decisions you make before anyone drills a hole.

## 1. Pick the Mount for How You'll Actually Watch

- **Fixed (flat) mounts** sit closest to the wall. Good when the TV is at eye level straight across from the couch.
- **Tilting mounts** angle the screen down a few degrees. Useful when the TV has to go a little higher than ideal, and it cuts glare from windows.
- **Full-motion (articulating) mounts** swing out and turn. Worth it for corner placements, open floor plans, or a TV you watch from the kitchen and the couch. They put the most leverage on the wall, so the anchoring matters most here.

Check two numbers on the mount box against your TV: the **VESA pattern** (the bolt spacing on the back of the TV) and the **weight rating**. If either one doesn't match, it's the wrong mount.

## 2. Height: Lower Than You Think

The most common mistake is mounting too high. For a TV you watch from a couch, the middle of the screen should land close to seated eye level. Above a fireplace, it's usually much higher than that, which is why a tilting or pull-down mount is often the better call there.

Before drilling, tape a piece of cardboard the size of the screen to the wall and sit down for a minute. It's a cheap way to avoid a second set of holes.

## 3. What's Behind the Wall Decides the Hardware

- **Drywall over wood studs** (most homes around Madison and Huntsville): the mount bolts into studs. Drywall anchors alone are not the place to hang a TV, especially on a full-motion arm.
- **Brick and concrete block:** these hold a TV well, but need masonry anchors and a hammer drill. Drill into the brick itself rather than the mortar joints when you can.
- **Tile:** use a tile bit, go slowly, and don't let the bit walk. A cracked tile behind a TV is a bigger repair than the mount.
- **Steel studs:** these show up in some newer construction and need different anchors than wood.

## 4. Over the Fireplace: Check the Heat First

Heat rising off a fireplace can shorten a TV's life. Before mounting above a mantel, light the fireplace, let it run a while, and feel the wall where the TV would go. If it's too warm to keep your hand on comfortably, that spot isn't right without a deeper mantel or a different location. Masonry fireplaces also mean masonry anchors, and sometimes a mount with extra reach to clear the mantel.

## 5. Hiding the Cables the Right Way

There are two clean options:

- **Surface raceway:** a paintable cord cover that runs down the wall. Quick and fully reversible.
- **In-wall concealment:** the low-voltage cables (HDMI, coax, network) run inside the wall. The TV's regular power cord should **not** be fished through the wall. That's what an in-wall power kit is for. It puts a proper recessed outlet behind the TV, connected to a power inlet near the floor.

Cutting into a wall also means checking first for anything already running inside it, like wiring or plumbing.

## When to Hand It Off

If it's a fixed mount on a drywall-and-stud wall and you own a stud finder and a level, it's a reasonable DIY job. Brick, tile, over-fireplace placement, full-motion arms on big screens, or in-wall cable work are where having someone who does it regularly pays off.
