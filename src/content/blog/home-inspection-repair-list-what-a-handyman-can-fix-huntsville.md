A home inspection report can run 40 pages and list dozens of items. That's normal. Most of them are small, and a good share are things a handyman can fix in a day or two. The trick is sorting the report into three piles: what a handyman can handle, what needs a licensed trade, and what you can safely ignore.

This comes up a lot in Huntsville and Madison because of how many homes change hands on short timelines, especially for military families with PCS orders and people relocating for jobs at Redstone Arsenal and Cummings Research Park.

## Items a Handyman Can Usually Fix

These are the inspection items we see most often, and they're squarely handyman work:

- **Drywall damage:** holes, cracks, nail pops, and water stains (after the leak itself is fixed). See our [drywall repair service](/services/drywall).
- **Doors that stick, don't latch, or are missing hardware**, including interior and exterior doors, door sweeps, and weatherstripping.
- **Loose or missing trim, baseboard, and caulk** around tubs, showers, sinks, and exterior windows.
- **Deck issues:** loose railings, soft or cracked boards, and missing fasteners. Structural problems like a failing ledger may need more work. See [deck repair](/services/deck-repair).
- **Gutters:** clogged, sagging, or disconnected gutters and downspouts that dump water at the foundation. See [gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning) and [gutter installation](/services/gutter-installation).
- **Fence and gate repairs:** leaning posts, broken pickets, and gates that won't latch.
- **Paint touch-ups:** peeling exterior trim, scuffed interior walls, and stained ceilings.
- **Damaged flooring:** lifted LVP planks, cracked tile, or loose transitions.
- **Missing or loose fixtures:** towel bars, cabinet hardware, handrails, and closet doors off their tracks.

## Items That Need a Licensed Trade

Some inspection items are not handyman work, and lenders or buyers often want a licensed contractor's invoice for them. These include:

- **Electrical:** double-tapped breakers, missing GFCI protection, open junction boxes, or panel issues. Call a licensed electrician.
- **Plumbing:** active leaks, water heater issues, or improper drain venting. Call a licensed plumber.
- **Roofing:** missing shingles, flashing problems, or active roof leaks. Call a roofer.
- **HVAC:** anything involving the system's operation or refrigerant.
- **Structural and foundation:** significant foundation cracks or settlement. Get a structural engineer's opinion first.

## Items You Can Often Ignore

Inspectors are thorough by design, and some items are notes rather than defects: cosmetic wear, older but working components, and recommendations to "monitor." Your agent can help you decide which ones matter in your negotiation.
