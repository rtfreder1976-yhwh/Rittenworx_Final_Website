After a leak, the question we hear most is "can I just let it dry and paint it?" Sometimes, but usually only if you caught it fast and the water was clean. Here's how to decide.

## What can usually be dried

- Drywall that was wet for a short time, still feels firm, and hasn't changed shape.
- A small area, dried within 24 to 48 hours with fans and a dehumidifier.
- Clean water from a supply line, as opposed to a slow, long-running leak or sewage.

Even then, check that the area is fully dry before closing it up or painting. Surface dry is not wall dry.

## What should come out

- Drywall that is soft, crumbling, sagging or bubbling.
- Stains that keep coming back or spread after it rains.
- Any area with a musty smell or visible mold growth.
- Anything that stayed wet for days. Wet insulation behind it has to go too.

Cut the wet section out a bit beyond the visible damage so you're patching into dry, solid board.

## Why you can't paint over it

Paint and stain-blocking primer cover a stain, they don't dry a wall. If moisture is still in the cavity, mold can grow behind the paint and the stain usually bleeds back through. The EPA says not to paint or caulk over mold.

## When it's a bigger job

Small areas of surface mold can be cleaned. Widespread mold, mold on porous materials, or any health concern is a job for a remediation specialist, and we'll tell you so rather than patch over it.

## The repair

Once the cause is fixed and the framing is dry, the rebuild is routine work: new board, taped and finished seams, texture matched to the rest of the room, then prime and paint.
